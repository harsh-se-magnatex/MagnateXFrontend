## 1. Add the DPA to the legal nav

The footer nav currently lists eight pages. Add a ninth link — `/legal/dpa` — to the nav component. Without it the page exists but nothing points at it, and the cross-references in Terms §14 and Privacy §15 will 404.

## 2. Licenses page needs code, not content

`licenses.md` is a **template with placeholders**, not a finished page. The package list must render from `src/data/licenses.json`, generated by `generate-licenses.mjs`.

```bash
mkdir -p scripts
cp generate-licenses.mjs scripts/
```

```json
"scripts": {
  "build": "node scripts/generate-licenses.mjs && next build"
}
```

The script has no dependencies, needs Node 18+, and writes both the JSON and the downloadable `third-party-notices.md`. It warns on packages with no declared license and on copyleft licenses, and fails the build in CI if a package ships with no license at all.

## 3. Product changes these pages now assume

Each of these is a statement the pages make. If the product doesn't match, the page becomes the same kind of liability the old ones were.

**Must be true before you publish:**

- **Grievance acknowledgment within 24 hours.** Terms §30, Privacy §13, AUP §8 and the AI Disclosure all now say 24 hours, not 48. The old 48-hour promise didn't meet the amended IT Rules. Make sure `founder@magnatex.co` is actually monitored to that standard.
- **GA4 fires only after consent.** Cookie Policy §3.3 states this explicitly, and it was already claimed (inaccurately) on your live Sub-processors page. Verify the banner genuinely gates the GA4 script and that Consent Mode is wired. This is the single most likely thing on the list to be false today.
- **Auto Approve acknowledgment.** Terms §11 says you elect Auto Approve knowingly. That's much stronger if the toggle itself shows a short confirmation the first time it's switched on — one sentence, one checkbox. Without it, "you agreed in the Terms" is a weaker argument.
- **EU/UK checkout consent.** Terms §25 and Refund §4 rely on you taking express consent at checkout to begin supply immediately, plus acknowledgment that the withdrawal right is lost. Dodo may or may not present this. Confirm with them; if not, add the checkbox on your side before redirect.
- **Chat assistant self-identifies as AI.** Article 50(1) has no grace period and applies now. If the assistant doesn't say so at first interaction, add it.

**Have a plan for, before 2 December 2026:**

- **C2PA content credentials.** AI Disclosure §7.2 commits to this. Your 28 July launch date buys you the transition period on Article 50(2) marking, but the clock is running. Keep dated evidence of that launch date — it's what the grace period depends on.
- Note that your compositing step (the HTML/CSS typography pass over the generated backdrop) is where provenance metadata is most likely to be stripped. Design for preservation there, not as an afterthought.

## 4. Two open items

**OpenBrand jurisdiction.** The Sub-processors row is marked *"see note below"* for location because I couldn't establish where Tight Studio is incorporated. Ask them for their entity name, country, and DPA, then fill it in.

Worth considering: OpenBrand is open-source with a self-hosting option (`github.com/tight-studio/openbrand`). If you self-host it, it stops being a sub-processor entirely — one fewer vendor, one fewer international transfer, one fewer row to keep current. For a service that only fires when a user submits a URL, that's a cheap simplification.

**SMTP relay.** Sub-processors currently says authentication email goes via Firebase and "other operational email is sent from our application via SMTP relay." Nodemailer is a library — whatever it authenticates against (Google Workspace, an SMTP provider, something else) is a sub-processor and needs naming. Fill that in.

## 5. What structurally changed

The old pages went stale because they hardcoded prices and plan names. Every number in the live Terms and Refund pages was wrong: Prime/Elite/Legacy at $24.99/$39.99/$59.99 against your actual AI and Studio lines, and $4.99–$39.99 top-ups against your actual packs.

The new pages state mechanics and point to the in-app billing page as authoritative. You can change pricing freely without touching legal copy. The only thing you now have to keep in sync is the sub-processor list — which is unavoidable, and is one page rather than four.

## 6. Before you publish

These drafts are thorough and current, but I'm not a lawyer. Two clauses carry enough weight to justify an hour of an Indian tech lawyer's time before they go live: the SCC incorporation in DPA §11 (the Module Two selections and the Irish-law choice), and the EU/UK withdrawal-right mechanics in Refund §4. Everything else is standard enough to ship and refine.

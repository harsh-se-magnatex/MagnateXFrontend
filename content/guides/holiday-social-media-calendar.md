## Why festival posts matter

Festivals and holidays are when people buy, gift and celebrate — and when they notice which brands showed up. A well-timed festival post does three things at once: it sells, it shows you're active, and it connects your brand with a moment your customers care about.

The mistake is remembering on the day. By then, the buying has already happened.

## Global dates worth planning for

| Occasion | When | Post ideas |
|---|---|---|
| New Year | 1 January | Year-in-review, new collections, resolutions |
| Lunar New Year | Late January–February | Celebration, red and gold themes |
| Valentine's Day | 14 February | Gift guides, couples, self-love |
| Mother's Day (US, UK and others) | US: second Sunday of May; UK: March | Gifts for mum, family stories |
| Father's Day (US and others) | US: third Sunday of June | Gifts for dad |
| Back to school | Late summer | Everyday essentials, routines |
| Halloween | 31 October | Playful themes, limited editions |
| Black Friday | Day after US Thanksgiving (late November) | Offers — only if you're actually running one |
| Cyber Monday | Monday after Black Friday | Online offers |
| Christmas | 25 December | Gift guides, festive collections |

## Indian festivals

Many of these follow the lunar calendar, so the date moves each year — check the current year's calendar when you plan.

| Festival | Usual timing | Post ideas |
|---|---|---|
| Holi | February–March | Color, joy, playful posts |
| Eid | Moves about 11 days earlier each year | Greetings, festive wear, gifting |
| Akshaya Tritiya | April–May | Gold and jewelry purchases, new beginnings |
| Raksha Bandhan | August | Sibling gifts |
| Independence Day (India) | 15 August | Pride, heritage, made-in-India |
| Ganesh Chaturthi | August–September | Greetings, traditional wear |
| Navratri | September–October | Nine-day themes, festive wear |
| Karva Chauth | October–November | Bridal and festive jewelry, couples |
| Dhanteras | Two days before Diwali | Gold, jewelry, auspicious purchases |
| Diwali | October–November | The biggest gifting season: collections, greetings, offers |
| Wedding season | Roughly November–February | Bridal collections, family gifting |

## How far ahead to plan

| Weeks before | Do this |
|---|---|
| 6–8 weeks | Choose which occasions matter to your customers. Plan the collection or offer. |
| 3–4 weeks | Create the posts: teaser, main post, follow-up. |
| 1–2 weeks | Publish teasers and gift guides. |
| On the day | Publish the greeting or main post. |
| After | Thank customers, share their photos. |

## Three posts per occasion

For each festival that matters to you, plan three posts:

1. **The teaser** — one to two weeks before: the collection, the gift guide, the offer.
2. **The main post** — on the day: a warm greeting that feels like your brand, not a stock card.
3. **The follow-up** — after: thank-you, customer photos, best-sellers from the season.

## Only pick the occasions that fit

You don't need to post for every holiday. Choose the ones your customers actually celebrate and that make sense for what you sell. A wedding jeweller should go big on wedding season and Dhanteras; a café might focus on New Year and local festivals.

## Frequently asked questions

### How early should I post about a festival?

Start teasers one to two weeks before, and plan the content six to eight weeks ahead. For big buying seasons like Diwali or Christmas, the earlier your gift guides go out, the more of the buying you catch.

### Should my business post for every holiday?

No. Post for the occasions your customers celebrate and that connect to what you sell. A few well-made festival posts beat a greeting for every date on the calendar.

### What should a festival greeting post include?

Your brand style, a warm message that sounds like you, and — where it fits — your products in the scene. Avoid generic stock greetings; they look the same as everyone else's.

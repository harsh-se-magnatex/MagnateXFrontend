## The short answer

| Platform | Starting frequency | Best formats |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram | 3–5 feed posts a week | Product photos, carousels, short videos |
| Facebook | 3–5 posts a week | Updates, offers, photos, events |
| LinkedIn (company page) | 2–3 posts a week | Expertise, news, case studies |

These are starting points. Your own data will tell you more within a month or two.

## Why consistency beats volume

Two businesses post 12 times a month. One posts three times a week, every week. The other posts daily for two weeks, then nothing for two weeks. The first business wins:

- **Followers learn when to expect you.** Regular posts keep you in their feed.
- **Your profile always looks active.** New visitors judge you by your most recent post date.
- **You learn faster.** Steady posting gives you comparable data week to week.

The usual failure isn't posting too little. It's posting in bursts — busy weeks go quiet, and the feed looks abandoned.

## Instagram

Instagram rewards visual quality and variety. A balanced week might be:

- one product post;
- one carousel (a collection, a how-to, a story);
- one short video;
- one post that shows proof or personality — a customer photo, a behind-the-scenes moment.

Mix formats rather than repeating one. Carousels and videos often earn more saves and shares than single images.

## Facebook

Facebook suits local and community-driven businesses. Updates, offers, events and photos all work. Three to five posts a week keeps a page active; more is fine if each post is genuinely useful.

## LinkedIn

On a company page, quality beats quantity. Two or three posts a week — expertise, company news, client results — is enough. For B2B businesses, posts from the founder's personal profile often reach further than the company page.

## What counts as a post

Feed posts, carousels and short videos all count toward your rhythm. Stories are extra — useful for day-to-day moments, but they don't replace feed posts, because they disappear after a day and don't build your profile.

## How to find your own rhythm

1. **Start with the table above** for one or two platforms.
2. **Hold it steady for a month.** Don't change frequency mid-test.
3. **Check reach and engagement per post.** If engagement per post holds as you post more, keep going. If it drops sharply, scale back.
4. **Post at your best hour.** Your analytics show when your followers are online. SocioGenie learns this from your own data and publishes at that time.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is posting every day on Instagram too much?

Not if every post is good and you can keep it up. But for most growing businesses, 3–5 strong posts a week beats 7 rushed ones. Quality and consistency matter more than daily volume.

### Does posting more often get more followers?

Up to a point. More good posts give more chances to be discovered, but engagement per post usually falls if quality drops. Increase frequency only while quality and engagement hold.

### What's the best time to post on social media?

It depends on when your followers are online, which varies by audience. Check your platform analytics for active hours, test a few slots for a month, and stick with what performs.

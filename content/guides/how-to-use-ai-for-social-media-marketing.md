## Before you start: decide what you want AI to do

There are two ways to use AI for social media, and it helps to choose one up front:

- **AI as an assistant.** You stay the operator. AI helps you write a caption or design an image, and you assemble and post everything yourself. Cheap, but it still takes your time every week.
- **AI as the operator.** AI plans the calendar, creates each post and publishes it. You give it context and approve what goes out. This is what actually takes social media off your plate.

The steps below work for both, but they matter most for the second.

## Step 1: Pick one or two platforms

Start where your customers already are. A product business — jewelry, fashion, food, interiors — usually starts with Instagram and Facebook. A B2B or professional business usually starts with LinkedIn. Doing one platform well beats spreading thin across four.

## Step 2: Give the AI real context

This is the step most people skip, and it is the reason most AI posts look generic. Before generating anything, give the AI:

- **Your website.** It holds your story, products, services and voice. A good tool reads it for you.
- **Product photos.** Real photos of what you sell — the AI should build posts around them, not invent products.
- **Your brand look.** Logo, brand colors and a few of your past posts or designs you like.
- **Your occasions.** The festivals, holidays and seasons your customers celebrate.
- **Your no-go list.** Claims you never want made, discounts you don't offer, words you avoid.

## Step 3: Generate samples and judge them honestly

Before committing to a month, look at real output for your brand. Check three things:

1. **Is the product exactly right?** Same stones, same colors, same logo.
2. **Does the caption sound like you?** Read it out loud.
3. **Would you stop scrolling for it?** If not, the design direction needs changing.

If the samples are close but not right, adjust the context — more photos, a clearer brand description, a different visual style — rather than editing every post by hand.

## Step 4: Plan the month

Let the AI build a full month, not one post at a time. A good plan mixes:

- product posts and ads;
- carousels that tell a short story;
- short videos;
- marketing visuals — your product on a billboard, a shop window or a magazine page;
- posts for the occasions in your calendar.

## Step 5: Choose how posts are reviewed

Never let AI publish to your brand unchecked at the start. Pick a review mode:

- **You review every post** before it goes live. Best for the first month.
- **Someone reviews for you.** On SocioGenie's AI Manager plans, Auto Approve routes each calendar post through SocioGenie's in-house team instead of you.

Loosen the review only once the output has earned your trust.

## Step 6: Connect accounts the safe way

Connect each account through the platform's own sign-in screen. Tools that use official APIs — Meta's Graph API for Instagram and Facebook, LinkedIn's API for company pages — never see your password, and you can revoke access at any time. Don't use any tool that asks for your password directly.

## Step 7: Publish at your best hour

Instead of guessing, let the AI learn from your analytics which hour gets the most engagement on each platform. Until there's enough data, use the time your audience is most likely online — evenings and lunch breaks are common starting points.

## Step 8: Review results and improve

At the end of each month, look at:

- your three best and three worst posts — and why;
- which post types got saves and shares, not just likes;
- profile visits, link clicks and messages.

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping context.** Generic input gives generic posts.
- **Publishing without review.** One invented discount can cost more trust than a month of posts earns.
- **Changing everything weekly.** Give a plan a full month before judging it.
- **Chasing every platform.** Spread thin, nothing performs.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the easiest way to start using AI for social media?

Start with one platform and an AI tool that learns your business from your website. Generate a few sample posts for your brand, review them honestly, and only then let it plan a full month.

### Do I need design skills to use AI for social media marketing?

No. Modern AI tools create the visuals from your product photos and brand colors. What helps most is knowing what you like — a few example posts or designs you admire make the output better.

### Can AI post to Instagram automatically?

Yes, through Meta's official Graph API. The tool creates the post and publishes it at the scheduled time, with no need to open Instagram. Make sure the tool uses the official API rather than asking for your password.

### How much time does AI save on social media?

Doing it all yourself typically takes five to ten hours a week for one or two platforms. With AI planning and creating posts, the work drops to providing context once and reviewing posts — often under an hour a week.

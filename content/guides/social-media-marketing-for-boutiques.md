## The boutique's challenge: too much stock, too little time

A boutique has the opposite problem to most businesses. There is always something new to show — new arrivals, restocks, seasonal pieces, sale items. The challenge is keeping up: if every new piece needs a photo, a caption and a post, the feed falls behind the stock within weeks.

The goal is a system where every collection gets seen without social media becoming a full-time job.

## Best platforms for boutiques

- **Instagram** — fashion's main platform: outfits, carousels, short videos, new-arrival posts.
- **Facebook** — local reach, store events, sale announcements, community.
- **LinkedIn** — rarely needed, unless you sell uniforms or corporate wear.

## What to post: ideas that work for fashion

1. **New-arrival carousels** — one drop, several pieces, swipeable.
2. **Outfit posts** — one hero piece styled three ways.
3. **Short try-on videos** — movement sells fabric and fit.
4. **Seasonal edits** — festive wear, summer, wedding guest, workwear.
5. **Size and fit guides** — answer the question every online buyer has.
6. **Fabric and detail close-ups** — prints, embroidery, finishes.
7. **Customer photos** — real people wearing your pieces.
8. **Behind the scenes** — sourcing, fittings, setting up the shop.
9. **Restock and best-seller posts** — social proof built in.
10. **Store-window and campaign visuals** — your collection presented like a big-brand campaign.

## Keep every collection visible

The trick is turning stock into content automatically:

- **Batch shoot each drop.** Photograph every new piece on arrival, flat and worn.
- **One drop, several posts.** A carousel, two outfit posts and a short video from the same shoot.
- **Recycle best-sellers.** Restyle them each season instead of letting them disappear.
- **Plan around seasons and festivals** — see the [holiday social media calendar](/guides/holiday-social-media-calendar).

## A sample month for a boutique

| Week | Posts |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | New-arrival carousel · try-on video · outfit post · fit guide |
| Week 2 | Customer photo · seasonal edit carousel · detail close-up |
| Week 3 | Outfit post · behind the scenes · best-seller restock |
| Week 4 | Festival or sale post · try-on video · month recap |

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should a boutique post on Instagram?

Three to five feed posts a week works for most boutiques, with extra posts around new drops, sales and festive seasons. Stories are useful extras for day-to-day updates.

### What is the best content for a fashion brand on Instagram?

Outfit and styling posts, try-on videos, new-arrival carousels and real customer photos tend to perform best — they show how pieces look and move on real people.

### How can a small boutique compete with big fashion brands on social media?

By being consistent, personal and local. Show real customers, real styling advice and the shop itself. AI tools now make big-brand-style visuals affordable, so presentation is no longer the gap it once was.

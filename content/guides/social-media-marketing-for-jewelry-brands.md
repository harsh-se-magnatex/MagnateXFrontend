## Why social media matters so much for jewelry

People buy jewelry with their eyes and their trust. Before visiting a store or ordering online, most customers look the brand up — and the feed is what they judge. A profile full of sharp, accurate photos of real pieces says "this is a serious jeweller". A quiet or blurry feed says the opposite.

## The best platforms for jewelry

- **Instagram** — the main stage. Close-ups, carousels and short videos of pieces being worn.
- **Facebook** — strong for local stores, offers, events and reaching older buyers.
- **LinkedIn** — only if you also sell B2B, for example wholesale or corporate gifting.

Start with Instagram and Facebook and do them well.

## What to post: 12 ideas that work for jewelry

1. **Macro close-ups** of one piece — stones, settings, finish.
2. **Worn shots** — the piece on a hand, ear or neckline, so buyers can picture it.
3. **Collection carousels** — one theme, four or five pieces, one story.
4. **Styling posts** — "three ways to wear this necklace".
5. **Bridal and wedding sets**, especially ahead of wedding season.
6. **Festival collections** — Diwali, Dhanteras, Akshaya Tritiya, Eid, Christmas.
7. **Gift guides** — by budget or by occasion: anniversaries, Valentine's Day, Mother's Day.
8. **Care tips** — how to store, clean and look after pieces.
9. **Craft and sourcing** — how a piece is made, where stones come from.
10. **Customer photos and reviews** — the strongest proof you have.
11. **New arrivals** — short videos of pieces catching the light.
12. **Marketing visuals** — your real piece on a billboard, in a shop window or on a magazine page.

## Plan around the moments people buy jewelry

Jewelry purchases cluster around occasions. Plan content six to eight weeks ahead of:

- **Wedding season** — bridal sets, matching pieces, family gifts;
- **Festivals** — Diwali, Dhanteras, Navratri, Karva Chauth, Akshaya Tritiya, Eid, Christmas;
- **Gifting days** — Valentine's Day, Mother's Day, anniversaries, New Year.

The full list, with timing, is in our [holiday social media calendar](/guides/holiday-social-media-calendar).

## Photography tips for jewelry posts

- **Light it softly.** Diffused window light shows sparkle without harsh reflections.
- **Use plain, textured backgrounds** — linen, marble, velvet — that don't compete with the piece.
- **Shoot close and sharp.** Buyers want to see the stones.
- **Show scale.** At least one shot on a hand, ear or neck.
- **Never misrepresent the piece.** Colors, stones and finish must be exactly true — this is the one thing jewelry buyers never forgive.

## The one rule for AI and jewelry: keep the product exact

AI is especially useful for jewelry because it can create beautiful scenes from one product photo. But it must never "improve" the piece: change a stone, shift a color, redraw a setting. Use a tool that keeps the real product untouched and generates only the scene around it — and review every post before it goes live.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should a jewelry store post on Instagram?

Three to five feed posts a week is a strong rhythm for most jewelry stores, with extra posts in the weeks before weddings and festivals. Consistency matters more than daily posting.

### What jewelry posts get the most engagement?

Close-up videos of pieces catching the light, worn shots, collection carousels and real customer photos tend to perform best — they show detail and help buyers imagine owning the piece.

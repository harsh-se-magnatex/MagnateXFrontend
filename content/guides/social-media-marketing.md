## What is social media marketing?

Social media marketing is the work of using social platforms to attract, persuade and keep customers. It covers three jobs:

- **Being seen** — regular posts that reach people who don't know you yet.
- **Being trusted** — an active, professional-looking profile that reassures people who are checking you out.
- **Being chosen** — giving people a clear next step: visit the shop, send a message, buy the product.

For most growing businesses, the second job matters more than people expect. A customer who hears about you, looks you up, and finds a profile that last posted four months ago often doesn't come back.

## Why it matters for a growing business

- **Your customers are already there.** Instagram, Facebook and LinkedIn are where people browse, compare and ask friends.
- **It compounds.** Every good post adds to a profile that keeps working for you long after it's published.
- **It's affordable to start.** You can do it with a phone and an hour a week — the real cost is consistency, not money.
- **It's proof.** An active feed of real products and real customers answers "is this business legitimate?" before anyone asks.

## Step 1: Pick your platforms

Don't try to be everywhere. Choose one or two platforms and do them properly.

| Platform | Best for | What works |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram | Products people buy with their eyes: jewelry, fashion, food, interiors, beauty | Strong visuals, carousels, short videos |
| Facebook | Local businesses, community, older audiences, events | Offers, updates, photos, community posts |
| LinkedIn | B2B, professional services, real estate, recruiting | Expertise, case studies, company news |

A jewelry store or boutique usually starts with Instagram and Facebook. A consultancy or real-estate firm usually starts with LinkedIn.

## Step 2: Know what to post

Most good feeds mix a handful of post types:

- **Product posts** — your products, beautifully shot, with a clear reason to care.
- **Carousels** — a short story across several slides: a collection, a how-to, a before-and-after.
- **Short videos** — product in motion, behind the scenes, customer reactions.
- **Occasion posts** — festivals, holidays and seasonal moments your customers celebrate.
- **Proof** — reviews, customer photos, milestones.
- **Expertise** — tips, care guides, styling ideas, answers to common questions.

A useful rule: for every post that sells, post two that help, entertain or show who you are. Need ideas for a specific trade? See our guides for [jewelry brands](/guides/social-media-marketing-for-jewelry-brands) and [boutiques](/guides/social-media-marketing-for-boutiques).

## Step 3: Decide how often to post

Consistency beats volume. A reasonable starting point for a growing business:

| Platform | Starting frequency |
|---|---|
| Instagram | 3–5 feed posts a week |
| Facebook | 3–5 posts a week |
| LinkedIn (company page) | 2–3 posts a week |

<!-- VERIFY: these are practical starting points, not platform rules; adjust if you have your own data. -->

Pick a rhythm you can keep for months, then adjust using your own analytics. The detail is in [how often to post on social media](/guides/how-often-to-post-on-social-media).

## Step 4: Plan a content calendar

A calendar turns good intentions into posts that actually go out. Plan a month at a time:

1. Mark the fixed dates first: festivals, holidays, launches, sales.
2. Fill the rest with a balanced mix of the post types above.
3. Batch the creation — make a week's posts in one sitting, not one a day.
4. Schedule everything in advance.

Our [social media content calendar guide](/guides/social-media-content-calendar) has a ready-made monthly template, and the [holiday social media calendar](/guides/holiday-social-media-calendar) lists the dates worth planning for.

## Step 5: Make it look like you

Customers should recognise your post before they read your name.

- Use the same brand colors, fonts and logo placement every time.
- Show your real products — never stock images that could belong to anyone.
- Write in one consistent voice: warm, premium, playful — whatever you are, stay it.
- Keep a small set of layouts and reuse them.

## Step 6: Measure what matters

Ignore vanity numbers at first. Watch:

- **Reach** — are new people seeing you?
- **Engagement rate** — do they care enough to like, comment, save or share?
- **Profile visits and link clicks** — are they taking the next step?
- **Messages and enquiries** — the closest signal to sales.

Review once a month. Keep doing what worked, stop what didn't, and try one new idea each month.

## Your 30-day plan

| Week | Do this |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | Pick one or two platforms. Update your profile photo, bio and link. Gather 20+ product photos. |
| Week 2 | Plan the month's calendar. Mark fixed dates. Create and schedule two weeks of posts. |

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best social media platform for a small or growing business?

The one your customers already use. Product businesses like jewelry, fashion and food usually start with Instagram and Facebook. Professional and B2B businesses usually start with LinkedIn. One platform done well beats three done badly.

### How long does social media marketing take to work?

Expect one to three months of consistent posting before you see clear trends in reach and engagement. Trust benefits start sooner: an active profile helps every customer who looks you up from day one.

### Do I need to pay for ads to succeed on social media?

No, not to start. Consistent, good organic posts build the profile and the proof. Ads work best later, to amplify posts you already know perform.

### How many hours a week does social media marketing take?

Doing it all yourself, five to ten hours a week for one or two platforms. With a freelancer or agency, one to two hours to brief and approve. With AI social media software that plans and creates for you, it can drop to reviewing posts — often under an hour a week.

### What should I post when I have nothing new to sell?

Show how your products are made or styled, share customer photos and reviews, answer common questions, celebrate the occasions your customers care about, and repost your best past content with a fresh angle.
